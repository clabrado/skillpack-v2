04 · Inverted analysis
What wasn't said, and how we lose

Reading the call backwards

Four lenses: absence, pre-mortem, customer-side and a claim audit.

Absence: signals that were missing

  • No infrastructure talk at all: no cloud, datacenter, hypervisor or Kubernetes
  • Acme never mentioned Datadog. The AE raised it. There's no stated observability pain.
  • No budget, timeline or decision process discussed
  • No other stakeholders. He'll only "consider" inviting others.
  • Wouldn't name his LLM stack ("doesn't matter which one"). Public signals point to one major vendor.

Read: this is an AI-governance and value buyer, not an ops buyer.

Pre-mortem: how this deal dies

  • Generic platform tour. That is exactly the "AI for its own sake" he warned against.
  • Over-promise caught by his "AI intake lead" vendor vetting (SOC 2, single intake)
  • Single-threaded on a director 9 months at Acme, with no ops budget, while IT defends Datadog
  • A replay of 2017 price shock: we lost twice on price to cheaper point tools
  • Muddled AI-eval partner story

Customer-side: what makes Hale the hero

  • He's a public voice on AI (conference panels, an enterprise-AI summit, trade press). He needs a story his executives believe.
  • "What did $8,000 of credits buy?" → give him a repeatable ROI artifact.
  • His posture is governance first ("AI intake lead"). Make Dynatrace the evidence layer behind his intake gate.
  • Small team → automation over headcount.

Strategic inversion: don't sell observability

The obvious play is "win back the APM seat from Datadog." Turned around: sell an AI control and value system that happens to be built on observability. Displacement follows once the platform is inside.

  • Land: AI workloads (net new, no incumbent fight)
  • Expand: logs and cost (Bindplane) → full stack via the reseller opp
